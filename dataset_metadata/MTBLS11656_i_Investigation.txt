Study Identifier	MTBLS11656
Study Title	Metabolomic Analysis Reveals an Important Role of Sphingosine 1-phosphate in the Development of HFMD due to EV-A71 Infection
Study Description	<p>Hand, foot, and mouth disease (HFMD) is a serious pediatric infectious disease that causes immeasurable physical and mental health burdens. Currently, there is a lack of information on the mechanisms of HFMD severity and early diagnosis. We performed metabolomic profiling of sera from 84 Enterovirus A71 (EV-A71) infections and 45 control individuals. Targeted metabolomics assays were employed to further validate some of the differential metabolic molecules. We identified significant molecular changes in the sera of HFMD patients compared to healthy controls (HC). A total of 54, 60, 35 and 62 differential metabolites were screened between mild cases and HC, severe cases and HC, severe cases and mild cases, and among the three groups, respectively. These differential metabolites implicated dysregulation of tricarboxylic acid cycle, alanine, aspartate and glutamate metabolism, and valine, leucine and isoleucine biosynthesis. The diagnostic panel based on overlapped differential metabolites could effectively discriminate severe cases from mild cases with an AUC of 0.912 (95% CI: 0.85~0.97) using Logistic regression model. Next, we found the elevation of serum sphingosine 1-phosphate (S1P) level in EV-A71 infection mice, which was similar to clinical observation. Importantly, after blocking the release of S1P by MK571, the clinical symptoms and survival of mice were significantly improved, involving with the reduce of leukocyte infiltration in infected brain tissues. Collectively, our data provided a landscape view of metabolic alterations in EV-A71 infected children, and revealed regulating S1P metabolism was an exploitable therapeutic target against EV-A71 infection.</p>

Study Protocol Description	<p>On the second day of admission, venous blood plasma of participants in the fasting state were collected for later use. Fasting blood plasma were also taken from healthy controls.</p>	<p>1. Thaw all samples at 4 °C; 2. Transfer 200 µL of each sample into 1.5 mL centrifuge tubes; 3. Add 800 µL of methanol (pre-cooled at -20 °C) to each tube and vortex for 60 s; 4. Centrifuge for 10 min at 12,000 rpm 4 °C and transfer all supernatant in each tube into another 1.5 mL centrifuge tube, samples were blow-dried by vacuum concentration; 5. Dissolve samples with 300 μL methanol aqueous solution (4:1, 4 °C), Filter through a 0.22 µm membrane and obtain the prepared sample extracts for LC-MS; 6. For the quality control (QC) samples, take 20 µL from each prepare sample extract and mix. 7. Use the rest of the samples for LC-MS test.</p>	<p>Chromatographic separation was accomplished in an Thermo Ultimate 3000 system equipped with an ACQUITY UPLC HSS T3 (150 x 2.1 mm, 1.8 µm, Waters) column maintained at 40 °C. The temperature of the autosampler was 8 °C. Gradient elution of analytes was carried out with 0.1% formic acid in water (A) and 0.1% formic acid in acetonitrile (B) or 5 mM ammonium formate in water (C) and acetonitrile (D) at a flow rate of 0.25 mL/min. Injection of 2 μL of each sample was done after equilibration. An increasing linear gradient of solvent B (v/v) was used as follows: 0.0-1.0 min, 2% B/D; 1.0-9.0 min, 2-50% B/D; 9.0-12.0 min, 50-98% B/D; 12.0-13.5 min, 98% B/D; 13.5-14.0 min, 98-2% B/D; 14.0-17.0 min, 2% B/D.</p>	<p>The ESI-MSn experiments were executed on the Thermo Q Exactive Focus mass spectrometer with the spray voltage of 3.8 kV and -2.5 kV in positive and negative modes, respectively. Sheath gas and auxiliary gas were set at 45 and 15 arbitrary units, respectively. The capillary temperature was 325 °C. respectively. The Orbitrap analyzer scanned over a mass range of 81-1000 m/z for full scan at a mass resolution of 70 000. Data dependent acquisition (DDA) MS/MS experiments were performed with HCD scan. The normalized collision energy was 30 eV. Dynamic exclusion was implemented to remove some unnecessary information in MS/MS spectra.</p>	<p>Proteowizard software (v3.0.8789) converts the obtained raw data into mzXML format (xcms input file format). The XCMS package of R (v3.3.2) was used for peaks identification, peaks filtration and peaks alignment. The main parameters are bw=5, ppm=15, peakwidth=c(10, 20), mzwid=0.015, mzdiff=0.01, method=centWave.</p>	<p>The names of the metabolites were identified by searching the online human metabolome database via aligning the molecular mass data (m/z). The dataset was scaled by the heatmap package in R (v3.3.2) to obtain a hierarchical cluster map of the relative quantitative values of metabolites.</p>
